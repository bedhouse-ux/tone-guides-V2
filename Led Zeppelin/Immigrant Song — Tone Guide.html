Tone Guide

Immigrant Song

Led Zeppelin — Led Zeppelin III (1970)
Key: B minor Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Driven, mid-forward power-chord riff

The Reference Point

This is a different Page than "Whole Lotta Love" — a Les Paul pushed into a cranked Marshall stack, tight and aggressive rather than fuzzed-out. Page reportedly ran a wah pedal locked in one fixed position for this track, using it as a static midrange voicing tool rather than sweeping it — less "wah effect," more permanent mid-forward snarl. No wah in the collection, but the EB-10's mid-boost can approximate that same fixed-position quack (see the pedal note below). The goal: tight low end, present mids, a driving amp that doesn't get muddy on the octave-jump riff.

58/15 LT humbuckers — the closest thing in the collection to a vintage Les Paul voice, which is exactly what this riff wants. Volume wide open for maximum drive into the amp. Tone nearly all the way up (9, not 10) to keep the attack crisp on the fast power-chord jumps — full open occasionally gets a touch dark on this pickup set. Bridge only; this riff doesn't need the neck pickup's warmth.

This carries the riff's crunch. 385+ voicing adds the extra gain stage for a thicker, more compressed saturation — closer to a pushed Marshall stack than the standard 385 mode. Channel A dialed as the main rhythm sound; Channel B is free to set up as a second gain stage later if you want an easy footswitch jump for the bridge section. Gain fairly high for sustain on the power chords, Treble kept present since the EB-10 downstream is doing the surgical mid work, not this pedal.

This is the closest substitute in the collection for Page's fixed-position wah trick. The Mid knob's Cut/Boost toggle set to Boost gives a permanent midrange push — the internal trim ships centered around 800Hz with a moderate Q, which lands in a similar zone to a wah rocked partway through its sweep and left there. Low and High left flat so the mid lift is the only thing happening. Flip the Boost switch (+10dB MOSFET) on for the solo if you want an extra lift on top of the 385's drive.

This is an approximation, not a reproduction — a static EQ boost and a wah pedal shape the frequency curve differently even when centered on the same frequency. It gets you in the neighborhood of that mid-forward snarl without an actual wah on the board.

Class A/B for the full 15W — this riff wants headroom and tightness, not the earlier breakup Class A gives you on the fuzz-driven songs. Bass Defeat engaged: it bypasses the 3-band EQ entirely (which is why Bass/Mid/Treble knobs aren't listed above — they're out of the circuit), trading it for a hairier, more open, louder voicing with sparklier highs and more punch. That's the right call here since the EB-10 is already handling the midrange shaping; you don't want the amp's own EQ fighting it. Bright switch on to keep the top end alive at the volumes this riff usually gets played at. Gain set high for that pushed-Marshall snarl.
