Rig Reference

Guitars, pedals, and amps — the source data every tone guide is built from
Last confirmed: August 2026

Guitars

All guitars run 10-46 (String Joy) strings, except the Taylor (acoustic set, gauge not logged).

Postmodern Telecaster

CuNiFe neck pickup, Nocaster '51 bridge pickup. The Nocaster bridge rates around 7.7K DC resistance / 3.7 Henries, neck around 7.9K / 2.35 Henries — period-correct single-coils delivering tight low end and clear, balanced midrange. Twangy, classic-rock-to-country bridge voicing. CuNiFe neck (cunife magnets rather than alnico) gives a fuller, slightly higher-output neck tone than a vintage-style Tele neck pickup.

Postmodern Stratocaster (2015)

Fat '60s pickup set — overwound design with beveled alnico 2 magnets, aiming for the sound of aged early-'60s pickups: warm lows, rich midrange, mellow highs. DC resistance around 6.7K, inductance 2.8 Henries across all three positions. Middle pickup is reverse-wound/reverse-polarity for hum cancelling in positions 2 and 4. Rounder and less icepick-y than a standard Strat set, with more midrange body.

Yamaha Revstar RS502T

Mahogany body with maple top, mahogany neck, rosewood fingerboard, 24.75" scale, 22 jumbo frets, two VP5 vintage-output P-90 pickups with Alnico V magnets, Tune-O-Matic bridge with custom aluminum tailpiece. Signature push-pull "Dry Switch" — a passive filter that cuts low frequencies for extra clarity and punch, giving coil-tap-like tonal shaping without actually splitting the coil. Punchy, articulate P90 voice with a useful thinning option.

Gibson Firebird (2022)

Mini-humbuckers, Grover mini tuners. Mini-humbuckers are traditionally low output, sitting between single-coil clarity and full-humbucker girth — a genuinely different circuit from a full-size PAF-style humbucker (originally sized to drop into P-90 routs). Bright, articulate, slightly glassy top end with less low-end push than the H150 or McCarty — cuts through a mix without getting muddy.

PRS McCarty

58/15 LT ("low turn") pickups — vintage-inspired, designed to capture the sweetness of vintage single coils and the warmth of late-1950s humbuckers: lively and open with focused midrange and balanced treble/bass. Mahogany body, maple top, ebony fretboard, Pattern neck profile. Essentially PRS's take on a great set of old PAFs — the closest thing in the collection to a vintage Les Paul voice.

Heritage H150 (2023, Dirty Lemon Burst)

Curly maple top over genuine Honduran mahogany back, Seymour Duncan '59 humbuckers (neck and bridge), wax-potted for minimal microphonic feedback — warm, thick tone with loads of sustain. 24¾" scale, 12" fretboard radius, Standard C neck profile. Bridge pickup with tone cranked and dirt engaged howls and snarls; neck pickup with tone rolled off gives thick humbucking warmth. Close cousin to the McCarty tonally but slightly darker/thicker, no coil-split.

Yamaha SA2200

Laminated sycamore body with a soft maple center block, Yamaha humbucking pickups with Alnico V magnets, mahogany neck, ebony fingerboard, Gotoh hardware. Push-pull coil-split on both tone pots, 3-way toggle. Regarded by many players as roughly 90% of an ES-335 for a fraction of the price. Semi-hollow warmth with real versatility via the coil splits — jazz-clean through to a growly 335-style crunch.

Epiphone Elitist Casino (2014)

Made at the Terada factory in Japan, dog-ear P90s. Fully hollow body construction (no center block), which makes the P-90s highly resonant and sensitive to feedback — the same characteristic Lennon, McCartney, and Richards leaned into creatively. Excellent articulation and harmonics with a distinct mid-range character. Bright, chimey, and physically loud acoustically — best kept away from high gain/close proximity to the amp.

Taylor 414ce-R

Grand Auditorium body, Indian rosewood back and sides, Sitka spruce top, V-Class bracing. Scooped midrange character with rich lows and pristine highs, sits well in arrangements with other instruments and vocals. Taylor Expression System 2 electronics, 25.5" scale. Excluded from tonal comparisons in the tone guides. Only guitar not on 10-46 String Joy strings.

Pedals

Board: Pedaltrain Novo  ·  Power: Friedman Power Station

On the Board — Front Chain

Peterson StroboStomp Board

Limited Edition. Tuner.

Walrus Audio Mira Board

Controls: Level, Blend, Attack, Release, Ratio, Make-Up, plus a side-chain High Pass Filter push-button (cuts frequencies below 120Hz from triggering compression).

Optical compressor. Blend allows mixing compressed signal with dry for clarity + sustain.

Wampler Tumnus Deluxe Board

Controls: Volume, Gain, Bass, Mids, Treble (active EQ), plus Normal/Hot (gain mode) and Buffer/True Bypass toggles.

Klon-style overdrive. Gain knob works as a dual-function control: turning gain up simultaneously reduces a parallel clean-blend circuit.

Boss BD-2 Blues Driver Board

Controls: Level, Tone, Gain.

Simple 3-knob overdrive. Tone shapes upper-mid/treble, Gain ranges from mild breakup to near-fuzz saturation.

Walrus Audio 385 Overdrive MkII Board

Controls: independent A/B Gain and Volume knobs (switchable via secondary footswitch), shared Treble and Bass EQ, and a 385/385+ voicing switch.

Dual-channel overdrive inspired by a vintage film projector's tube output stage. 385+ adds an extra gain stage for thicker, more compressed saturation.

On the Board — Effects Loop

Walrus Audio EB-10 Loop

Controls: Low, Mid, High rotary knobs (each with Cut/Boost toggle, up to 12dB each), a Boost switch (+10dB MOSFET), Live/Preset mode with 3 storable presets.

Preamp/EQ/boost. Internal trim pots adjust mid-band center frequency (ships at 800Hz, Q ~1.5) and bandwidth.

UAFX Brigade Chorus & Vibrato Loop

Controls: Speed, Depth, Intensity, Shade, Shape, Mode knobs; switches for effect mode, Store, and A/B variation.

Modulation.

UAFX Starlight Echo Station Loop

Controls: tap tempo, Live and Preset modes.

Delay. Three onboard models: Tape EP-III, Analog DMM, Precision.

UAFX Golden Reverberator Loop

Controls: Decay and Predelay (vary by selected effect), Live and Preset modes.

Reverb. Modeled types: Fender spring-style (Dream '65-derived), EMT 140 plate, Lexicon 224.

Boss RC-5 Loop

Looper.

On the Shelf

Keeley Seafoam Shelf

Controls: Rate, Depth, Tone, Blend.

Analog-digital chorus/vibrato. Blend dials the relationship between vibrato (fully counter-clockwise) and pure chorus (clockwise); Tone shifts the voicing from mellow/warm to bright shimmer; opto-coupler-controlled LFO gives smooth, deep modulation at slow rates. True bypass.

Keeley Hydra Shelf

Controls: Reverb side — Dwell, Mix, Color, 3-way Spring/Plate/Room toggle. Tremolo side — Rate, Depth, 3-way Sine/Harmonic/Vibrato toggle, tap tempo.

Stereo reverb & tremolo. Independent footswitches per effect, switchable effect order (trem→verb or verb→trem), 3 onboard presets, true or buffered trails bypass.

Funny Little Boxes 1991 (Gold Sparkle) Shelf

Controls: 2 Gain knobs, 3-band EQ, Volume.

Op-amp overdrive, voiced after Pearl Jam's debut-era tone (mid-forward, sparkly top, easy on bass).

Boss DD-7 Shelf

Controls: E.Level, F.Back (feedback), D.Time, Mode knob (4 time ranges — 50/200/800/3200ms — plus Hold/looper up to 40s, Modulate, Analog/DM-2 mode, Reverse).

Digital delay. Stereo I/O, tap tempo via external footswitch, expression pedal input for real-time Time/Feedback/Level control.

Way Huge Swollen Pickle MK3 Shelf

Controls: Loudness, Filter (tone), Sustain (fuzz/gain), plus Scoop and Crunch toggles.

Muff-style fuzz. Scoop carves out mids for a huge/flat power-chord voicing; Crunch adjusts fuzz compression intensity — both switches down gives the classic 3-knob Swollen Pickle voicing.

Mozztronics GD-1 Bonebreaker Shelf

Germanium-voiced overdrive, five modes. Best placed early in a chain — sensitive to input impedance, gets fizzy/undefined when stacked after other drives.

JHS Cheese Ball Shelf

Multi-mode fuzz, Lovetone Big Cheese recreation with four voicings.

MXR Super Badass Distortion Shelf

Controls: Output, Distortion (gain), Bass, Mid, Treble.

Full 3-band active EQ rather than a single Tone knob, so it can range from low-gain '70s-style overdrive through to scooped modern metal distortion. True bypass.

MXR FOD Drive Shelf

Controls: independent Gain/Volume for High Gain and Crunch Gain channels, Blend, Tone, Scoop switch (flat/scooped/boosted midrange).

Dual-channel overdrive recreating two blended amp stacks.

Keeley Compressor Mini Shelf

Controls: Level (output volume) and Comp (compression threshold) only — attack, release, and ratio are fixed internally.

Parallel/auto-blend compression (dry signal sums with compressed signal) plus automatic tone-recovery circuit to retain high end at heavy compression. Unity gain typically falls around 30-40% of the Level knob's range.

Fender Santa Ana Overdrive Shelf

Controls: Drive, Bass, Middle, Treble, Presence, Output Level, Voice toggle (A/B), plus a Boost footswitch.

Voice A is tighter/natural overdrive, B is smoother/thicker for leads. Boost footswitch mode (Drive or Level) is set internally.

Warm Audio Warm Bender New — In Transit

Tone Bender MkII-style fuzz, three NOS germanium/silicon modes.

Walrus Audio Luminary V2 New — In Transit

Controls: four independent octave-level knobs (-2, -1, +1, +2), Dry/Wet blend, Attack, Filter, Flutter. Three onboard presets plus Live mode, expression pedal input, Smart Momentary Bypassing.

Polyphonic quad octave generator, capable of stacking four octaves simultaneously rather than a single-voice pitch shift, running on the Analog Devices SHARC platform for fast, clean tracking. Dry/Wet: below 12 o'clock keeps the octave signal subtle under the dry guitar, above it the octaves dominate. Attack dictates how fast the octaves swell in after picking — low/off for immediate response, higher for a gradual bloom. Filter is a low-pass cutoff on the octave signal that doubles as an envelope-type effect at higher settings. Flutter applies tremolo depth/rate to the octaves, off at zero. Expression input is assignable to octave levels, Attack, Flutter, and/or Dry/Wet in combination.

Amps

Frost Amplification Clapp 15

Hand-built, Hobart/Margate, Tasmania (Tim Krushka). 1x12" Class A push-pull combo with a genuine Constant Current Source (CCS) keeping the output stage true Class A — most "Class A" push-pull amps are actually A/B without a CCS.

Controls: Class A / Class A-B switch (Class A limits output to 9W, more compression/sag; A/B unlocks the full 15W) · Bass, Mid, Treble (3-band EQ) · Bass defeat switch (bypasses the 3-band EQ for a hairier, louder, more open voicing) · Bright switch (boosts treble at lower volumes) · Pre-Amp Gain and Master Volume, independently controllable · Tremolo (front panel or footswitch) · Effects Loop (hosts EB-10, Brigade, Starlight, Golden Reverberator, RC-5).

Cascode preamp input stage gives Vox/pentode-style dynamics without the feedback/microphony issues real pentode preamps suffer. 12AX7 and 12AU7 preamp valves, EL84 (or Russian 6P14P) output valves. Single channel, no built-in reverb. 12" lightweight neodymium speaker on-board.

Quilter Aviator Cub

Solid-state (Quilter's amp-modeling power/preamp technology), 1x12" combo, 50W into a custom Quilter/Eminence-designed 12" speaker. Three separate input jacks, each voiced after a different vintage Fender combo — plug into the input matching the voice you want.

TweedLate-1950s tweed. Fat, honest, pre-tone-stack, plenty of gain for rich overdrive.
BlondeEarly-1960s blonde/brown. Wider treble boost, clear bell-like tone, more headroom than Tweed.
Blackface1965 black-panel. Scooped mids, sweet silky highs, full bass — benchmark clean American tone.

Controls: Gain · Bass, Middle, Treble (active EQ — Bass affects everything below ~500Hz, most aggressive below 200Hz) · Limiter/threshold ("soft ceiling" that caps overdrive without killing sustain) · Reverb (single-knob level) · Master/Speaker Volume, independent of preamp gain · SIG OUT (headphone/balanced line out level) · Effects Loop (send/return, mono).

Lightweight (21.5 lbs), full-range/flat power section — the amp character comes entirely from the three voiced preamp inputs, not tube sag or output-stage coloration, so it behaves more predictably at any volume than the Clapp 15. Good clean/low-gain pedal platform, especially via Blackface for headroom or Tweed for pre-broken-up grit.

Kemper Player (Level 3)

Digital amp/effects profiler in a compact pedalboard-style unit — outputs into an FRFR speaker, powered cab, PA, or headphones/interface. Uses Kemper's profiling technology to reproduce captured "fingerprints" of real amps rather than modeled approximations.

Level 3 adds: the full signal chain used on the larger Profiler models — doubles effects modules from 4 to 8, adds a Parallel Path (splittable/dual signal routing), Morphing, more Performance slots, expanded preset/rig management. GAIN knob LED collar glows blue at Level 3 (green = Level I, red = Level II).

Controls: two assignable footswitch-adjacent knobs (each doubles as a push-button to toggle two assigned parameters) · Bluetooth and Wi-Fi (can run simultaneously) · 6-button Bank/Rig selection row · tap tempo/tuner button · multi-colour input-level LED · 3 tuner LEDs · 3 footswitches (prev/next rig, one assignable) · built-in tuner, transpose/pitch, USB audio, MIDI, expression pedal input.

Deep editing lives in Rig Manager software (Windows/macOS) — the Player's onboard interface is deliberately minimal. Profiles are generally rated very high for tone accuracy, with the caveat that profiled EQ won't always respond like the real amp's controls if pushed far from where it was profiled. Best paired with a genuine FRFR speaker (the FR-10 below) for accurate reproduction.

Fender Tone Master FR-10 (FRFR monitor)

Powered full-range, flat-response speaker — reproduces whatever the Kemper Player sends it without adding its own amp/cab colouration. The playback partner for the Kemper Player rig, not a tone source in its own right.

Power1,000W Class-D
Drivers10" LF + 1" HF compression
Freq. response75Hz–20kHz (-3dB)
Max output130dB
Weight~25.6 lbs (11.6kg)

Controls: Volume, Treble, Middle, Bass, High-Frequency Cut (3-band active EQ + HF cut) · one combo input (balanced 1/4"/XLR) · one XLR line out with ground lift · integrated tilt-back legs.

Because it's genuinely flat/full-range, tone shaping should happen on the Kemper (profile, onboard EQ, effects) rather than on the FR-10 — its own EQ is for room correction/taste, not for building the tone itself.

File Organization

Convention for saving song tone guides to Google Drive (Tone Guides folder).

Folder structure: Tone Guides/[Artist]/

File naming, per song:

  • [Song] — Tone Guide.html
  • [Song] — Rhythm Tab.pdf
  • [Song] — Lead Tab.pdf

Tabs are saved manually from Ultimate Guitar using the filename above — Claude can't reach into the account or rename files after the fact, it just states what to name it on save.

Workflow, every time a tone guide is built:
  1. Before building, ask which rig to build for — Digital (Kemper Player + FR-10), Clapp 15, or Quilter Aviator Cub. Wait for confirmation, don't assume.
  2. Once a guide is finished, save it to Drive by default — no need to ask each time. Say so if a guide shouldn't be saved.
  3. Before saving, ask whether YouTube links should be added — get the actual link(s) or search and confirm candidates. Don't guess.
  4. YouTube links go in the tone guide HTML itself, not as separate files.
  5. For a song with an existing guide, always ask whether this is a new build or an edit/addition before creating a new file or overwriting.
