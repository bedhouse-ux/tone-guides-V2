Tone Guide

Teenage Dirtbag

Wheatus — Wheatus (2000)
Key: E Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Bright, punchy pop-punk crunch

The Reference Point

This is turn-of-the-millennium pop-punk/alt-rock: a picked verse riff with open high strings ringing over fretted low notes needs to stay clear and articulate, while the power-chord chorus wants real crunch and snap. The tone has to do both jobs without a pedal change — clean up under a light touch, bite when you dig in. Bright and punchy rather than thick or dark, with just enough grit to feel driven instead of polite.

Guitar

Postmodern Telecaster

Pickup: Bridge (Nocaster '51)
10

8
Tone

The Nocaster '51 bridge pickup gives tight low end and clear, balanced midrange — exactly the separation this riff needs, since the verse depends on open high strings ringing distinctly against fretted notes on the lower strings rather than blurring together the way a humbucker or P90 tends to. Volume wide open for a full, consistent signal into the BD-2. Tone at 8 keeps some brightness in reserve rather than going fully open, so the chorus power chords stay punchy without turning harsh once the drive kicks in.

Gain kept moderate rather than maxed — this needs to clean up on the lighter-touch verse picking and only really bite when you dig in on the chorus power chords, and a lower gain setting preserves more of that touch-sensitivity than cranking it. Tone pushed brighter than the darker blues-rock guides in this library (6.5 vs. the low-4 settings used elsewhere) since this song wants snap and presence, not thickness. Level set to give the BD-2 a genuine push into the front of the Clapp 15 rather than just adding color.

Not Used

Walrus Audio Mira

Bypassed for this song.

This riff's dynamic contrast between the picked verse and the driven chorus is part of the arrangement — compressing it flat would blur exactly the distinction the tone is built around.

Class A/B for headroom and a tighter low end — this song wants punch and clarity, not the earlier sag and compression Class A gives on the more vintage blues-rock builds. Treble pushed up and the Bright switch engaged together for that poppy, present top end; Bass kept moderate so the power chords don't get boomy. Pre-Amp Gain set to a genuinely moderate level, since the BD-2 is supplying most of the drive — the amp's job here is to stay clean-adjacent and let the pedal do the crunching.
