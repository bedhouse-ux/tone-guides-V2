Thickfreakness

The Black Keys — Thickfreakness (2003)
Key: A Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Raw, cranked, basement-recorded garage blues

The Reference Point

This whole record was cut live in Auerbach's basement, and the guitar tone is basically a cheap hollow-body single-coil guitar shoved through a small amp that's being asked to do more than it comfortably can. It's not a clean fuzz pedal tone — it's amp breakup, thick and a little ragged at the edges, with almost no studio polish on top. The goal here is that same character: raw, mid-thick, slightly loose, driven hard rather than boosted clean.

Guitar

Epiphone Elitist Casino

Pickup Selector: Bridge

Bridge Tone

Fully hollow body, dog-ear P90s — this is the closest thing in the collection to the cheap resonant hollow-body single-coil guitars Auerbach actually used on this record. It won't be identical to a department-store Silvertone or Guyatone, but the loose, slightly wild hollow-body character and P90 midrange bark are in the same family. Bridge pickup for grit and cut. Volume wide open to push the amp hard. Tone rolled back to 6, not wide open — P90s can get a little icepick-y at full tilt, and this recording is thick and dark rather than bright.

This guitar is genuinely loud and resonant acoustically, and feedback-prone at the volumes this song wants — keep an eye on proximity to the amp, same caution as usual with the Casino.

Everything used is already in its normal board position. Effects loop stays empty for this one — no reverb, no delay, nothing. The original recording is bone dry, and stacking any ambience on top works against the raw, right-in-your-face character.

Compression evens out the raggedness that makes this tone work. This riff wants uneven, slightly loose picking dynamics coming through — leave it off.

The BD-2's asymmetrical clipping is voiced to feel like a pushed tube amp rather than a stompbox — which is exactly the character this song needs, feeding into an amp that's also being driven hard. Gain set high for real saturation, not just a light edge-of-breakup boost. Level pushed up to really slam the front of the Clapp 15. Tone kept low-ish (4) rather than bright — this tone is thick and a little dark, not scratchy or fizzy on top.

Not Used

Walrus Audio 385 Overdrive MkII

Bypassed for this song.

The 385's voicing is more modern/Marshall-leaning than this track wants — the BD-2 alone into a cranked amp gets closer to the loose, low-fi basement character than stacking a second, cleaner-voiced drive would.

Class A mode — a small amp being pushed past comfortable is basically the whole story of this record, and Class A's earlier breakup and sag gets you there faster than the cleaner headroom of A/B. Gain set high, close to the top of its useful range, so the amp itself is contributing real saturation on top of the BD-2, not just passing the pedal's signal through clean. Bass pushed up and Mid pushed up together for that thick, chesty low-mid weight; Treble pulled back so nothing gets thin or fizzy. Bass Defeat stays off here — you want the 3-band EQ actively shaping this into something dark and thick, not the more open/hairy voicing Bass Defeat gives you. Bright switch off for the same reason.
