If You Love Her

Tokyo Tea Room — No Future Plans (2022)
Key: A minor Rig: Digital — Kemper Player + FR-10 Feel: Dreamy, hypnotic psych-pop clean

The Reference Point

This is a psych-pop track built on Am7–Em7–Fmaj7–Dm7 throughout, with a description straight from the band themselves: "melodic bass lines, hypnotic guitar riffs and dreamy synths." That's a clean-to-barely-breaking-up guitar sitting in a bed of modulation and space — chorus for shimmer, delay for the hypnotic repeating quality, reverb for depth — not a gain-driven tone at all.

The Fat '60s pickup set is warmer and less icepick-y than a standard Strat set, which suits this — you want the jangle without the edge. Position 2 (neck+middle, hum-cancelling) gives a round, glassy clean that still has enough top-end sparkle to catch the chorus and shimmer well. Tone rolled back slightly from full so the 7th-chord voicings (Am7, Em7, Fmaj7, Dm7) don't get glassy/thin under modulation.

On the current setup, the loop-based modulation/delay/reverb pedals (Brigade, Starlight, Golden Reverberator, EB-10) are wired into the Clapp 15's effects loop, not the Kemper Player. For the Digital rig, chorus/delay/reverb come from the Kemper's own internal effects slots instead — Level 3 gives you 8 of them to work with. The FR-10 should stay flat; it's there to reproduce what the Kemper sends, not to shape tone itself.

Profile choice: reach for a clean Fender Blackface-style rig if you've got one in your library — that scooped-mid, sweet-top clean character is the standard reference point for this kind of jangly, chorus-heavy indie/psych-pop tone. I don't have visibility into your saved rig library on the Player, so I can't point to a specific profile by name — pick the cleanest, clearest Fender-style profile you've got and start there.

Gain kept low — this should stay essentially clean, with just enough on tap that the loudest strums round off slightly rather than staying glassy-clean throughout. These knob values are Amp Block parameters as edited in Rig Manager; the Player's onboard controls are deliberately minimal, so this level of detail lives in the software, not on the unit.

This is doing most of the "hypnotic" work — a moderate-depth, slow-ish chorus gives that shimmering, slightly liquid movement without turning the pitch seasick. Mix kept well under 50% so the dry signal still anchors the chord voicings clearly.

Delay

~330ms
Time
3

Mix

A dotted-eighth-feeling delay at this tempo (90 BPM) gives the repeating, hypnotic quality the band describes without needing to play more notes than the riff actually calls for. Low feedback — one or two clear repeats, not a wash. A tape or analog-voiced delay algorithm fits the warm, slightly degraded character better than a pristine digital repeat here.

Reverb

Hall or Plate Reverb (onboard effects slot)

2.5s
Decay
20%
Mix

A longer, lusher decay than you'd use on a rock rhythm tone — this genre wants space and dreaminess. Plate for a smoother, more modern sheen, or Hall if you want more sense of physical size; either works, it's more a taste call than a technical one here.
