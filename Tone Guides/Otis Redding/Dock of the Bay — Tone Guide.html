Dock of the Bay

Otis Redding — The Dock of the Bay (1968)
Key: G Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Warm, relaxed, clean rhythm strum

The Reference Point

Steve Cropper's original tone on this is dry, clean, and unhurried — a warm Fender-style strum sitting well back in the mix behind Otis Redding's vocal, whistling, and the wave sounds. Nothing in the collection is a literal match for Cropper's Telecaster-through-tweed sound, but the goal is the same character: gentle, clean, and easy, never pushing or crowding the arrangement. This is also one of the better songs in the practice plan for rotating through open chords and both E-shape and A-shape barre chords in one progression, so a clean tone that doesn't mask any sloppiness in the chord changes is doing double duty.

Of what's in the collection, the 58/15 LT pickups get closest to this song's warm, easy character — open and focused in the midrange without the scoop of a hollow-body or the glassiness of a bright single-coil. Neck pickup rounds it out further, since this needs warmth and roundness over cut or twang. Volume backed off slightly from full (9) to keep the pickup from driving the front end of the amp too hard, and Tone at 6 for a softer top end that sits gently rather than sparkling.

Class A/B for genuine headroom and a properly clean tone throughout — this song has no room for the earlier breakup or sag Class A introduces, even lightly. Pre-Amp Gain kept low so the amp stays clean at any reasonable playing volume. Treble pulled back slightly from center and Bright left off, since this wants warmth rather than sparkle; Bass and Mid both moderate for a full, rounded strum that doesn't crowd the vocal.
