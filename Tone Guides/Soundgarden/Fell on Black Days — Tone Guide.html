Tone Guide

Fell on Black Days

Soundgarden — Superunknown (1994)
Key: E Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Thick, heavy, unhurried grunge riff in 6/4

The Reference Point

This riff is built on E5–B5–Cmaj7 power chords/triads and needs to feel thick, heavy, and unhurried — the 6/4 time signature means it shouldn't feel rushed or choppy even though it's a driving riff. Cornell himself noted the drums play it straight, which is part of what keeps the riff from feeling quirky once you're locked in. The bridge opens up into a more melodic single-note line, which wants a rounder, warmer character than the riff's snarl.

Guitar

Heritage H150

Pickup: Bridge for riff, Neck for bridge-section melody
10

7.5
Tone

Seymour Duncan '59 humbuckers give real thickness and sustain, which is exactly the character this riff wants — closer to the original's heavier tone than anything brighter in the collection would give. Bridge pickup with tone cranked for the main riff, where the H150 genuinely howls and snarls when pushed. Switch to the neck pickup with tone rolled back for the bridge section's melodic single-note line, for thick, warm humbucking character that separates it from the riff.

385+ voicing for the extra gain stage, giving a thicker, more compressed saturation that suits this riff's weight better than the standard 385 mode. Gain set high for sustain and thickness on the power chords. Bass pushed up and Treble kept moderate rather than bright — this tone is heavy and dark, not scratchy on top, in line with the mid-90s Seattle sound.

Class A/B for real headroom under the 385's drive — this riff wants weight and control, not the looser sag Class A would add on top of an already-driven pedal. Bass and Mid both pushed up for that thick, heavy low-mid presence; Treble held back so nothing gets thin or fizzy. Bass Defeat and Bright both stay off — the 3-band EQ is doing real work keeping this dark and controlled rather than opening it up.
