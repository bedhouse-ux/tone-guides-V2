# Guitars

*Strings: all guitars run 10-46 (String Joy), except the Taylor (acoustic set, not specified)*

### 1. Postmodern Telecaster
CuNiFe neck pickup, Nocaster '51 bridge pickup. The Nocaster bridge rates around 7.7K DC resistance / 3.7 Henries, neck around 7.9K / 2.35 Henries — period-correct single-coils delivering tight low end and clear, balanced midrange. Twangy, classic-rock-to-country bridge voicing. CuNiFe neck (cunife magnets rather than alnico) gives a fuller, slightly higher-output neck tone than a vintage-style Tele neck pickup.

### 2. Postmodern Stratocaster (2015)
Fat '60s pickup set — overwound design with beveled alnico 2 magnets, aiming for the sound of aged early-'60s pickups: warm lows, rich midrange, mellow highs. DC resistance around 6.7K, inductance 2.8 Henries across all three positions. Middle pickup is reverse-wound/reverse-polarity for hum cancelling in positions 2 and 4. Rounder and less icepick-y than a standard Strat set, with more midrange body.

### 3. Yamaha Revstar RS502T
Mahogany body with maple top, mahogany neck, rosewood fingerboard, 24.75" scale, 22 jumbo frets, two VP5 vintage-output P-90 pickups with Alnico V magnets, Tune-O-Matic bridge with custom aluminum tailpiece. Has the signature push-pull "Dry Switch" — a passive filter that cuts low frequencies for extra clarity and punch, giving coil-tap-like tonal shaping without actually splitting the coil. Punchy, articulate P90 voice with a useful thinning option.

### 4. Gibson Firebird (2022)
Mini-humbuckers, Grover mini tuners. Mini-humbuckers are traditionally low output, sitting between single-coil clarity and full-humbucker girth — a genuinely different circuit from a full-size PAF-style humbucker (originally sized to drop into P-90 routs). Bright, articulate, slightly glassy top end with less low-end push than the H150 or McCarty — cuts through a mix without getting muddy.

### 5. PRS McCarty
58/15 LT ("low turn") pickups — vintage-inspired, designed to capture the sweetness of vintage single coils and the warmth of late-1950s humbuckers: lively and open with focused midrange and balanced treble/bass. Mahogany body, maple top, ebony fretboard, Pattern neck profile. Essentially PRS's take on a great set of old PAFs — the closest thing in the collection to a vintage Les Paul voice.

### 6. Heritage H150 (2023, Dirty Lemon Burst)
Curly maple top over genuine Honduran mahogany back, Seymour Duncan '59 humbuckers (neck and bridge), wax-potted for minimal microphonic feedback — warm, thick tone with loads of sustain. 24¾" scale, 12" fretboard radius, Standard C neck profile. Bridge pickup with tone cranked and dirt engaged howls and snarls; neck pickup with tone rolled off gives thick humbucking warmth. Close cousin to the McCarty tonally but slightly darker/thicker, no coil-split.

### 7. Yamaha SA2200
Laminated sycamore body with a soft maple center block, Yamaha humbucking pickups with Alnico V magnets, mahogany neck, ebony fingerboard, Gotoh hardware. Push-pull coil-split on both tone pots, 3-way toggle. Regarded by many players as roughly 90% of an ES-335 for a fraction of the price. Semi-hollow warmth with real versatility via the coil splits — jazz-clean through to a growly 335-style crunch.

### 8. Epiphone Elitist Casino (2014)
Made at the Terada factory in Japan, dog-ear P90s. Fully hollow body construction (no center block), which makes the P-90s highly resonant and sensitive to feedback — the same characteristic Lennon, McCartney, and Richards leaned into creatively. Excellent articulation and harmonics with a distinct mid-range character. Bright, chimey, and physically loud acoustically — best kept away from high gain/close proximity to the amp.

### 9. Taylor 414ce-R
Grand Auditorium body, Indian rosewood back and sides, Sitka spruce top, V-Class bracing. Scooped midrange character with rich lows and pristine highs, sits well in arrangements with other instruments and vocals. Taylor Expression System 2 electronics, 25.5" scale. Only guitar in the collection not on 10-46 String Joy strings (acoustic phosphor bronze/similar, gauge not logged here).

# Pedals

*Board: Pedaltrain Novo*
*Power: Friedman Power Station*

## On the Board

### Front chain
1. **Peterson StroboStomp (Limited Edition)** — tuner
2. **Walrus Audio Mira** — optical compressor. Controls: Level, Blend, Attack, Release, Ratio, Make-Up, plus a side-chain High Pass Filter push-button (cuts frequencies below 120Hz from triggering compression). Blend allows mixing compressed signal with dry for clarity + sustain.
3. **Wampler Tumnus Deluxe** — Klon-style overdrive. Controls: Volume, Gain, Bass, Mids, Treble (active EQ), plus two toggle switches — Normal/Hot (gain mode) and Buffer/True Bypass. Gain knob works as a dual-function control: turning gain up simultaneously reduces a parallel clean-blend circuit.
4. **Boss BD-2 Blues Driver** — overdrive. Controls: Level, Tone, Gain. Simple 3-knob layout; Tone shapes upper-mid/treble, Gain ranges from mild breakup to near-fuzz saturation.
5. **Walrus Audio 385 Overdrive MkII** — dual-channel overdrive inspired by a vintage film projector's tube output stage. Controls: independent A/B Gain and Volume knobs (switchable via secondary footswitch), shared Treble and Bass EQ, and a 385/385+ voicing switch (385+ adds an extra gain stage for thicker, more compressed saturation).

### Effects loop
6. **Walrus Audio EB-10** — preamp/EQ/boost. Controls: Low, Mid, High rotary knobs (each with a Cut/Boost toggle, up to 12dB each), a Boost switch (+10dB MOSFET boost), and Live/Preset mode switching with 3 storable presets. Internal trim pots adjust mid-band center frequency (ships at 800Hz, Q ~1.5) and bandwidth.
7. **UAFX Brigade Chorus & Vibrato** — modulation. Controls: Speed, Depth, Intensity, Shade, Shape, Mode knobs; switches for effect mode, Store, and A/B variation.
8. **UAFX Starlight Echo Station** — delay. Three onboard delay models (Tape EP-III, Analog DMM, Precision), with tap tempo, Live and Preset modes.
9. **UAFX Golden Reverberator** — reverb. Modeled reverb types include Fender spring-style (Dream '65-derived), EMT 140 plate, and Lexicon 224. Live and Preset modes; Decay and Predelay controls vary by selected effect.
10. **Boss RC-5** — looper.

## On the Shelf

- **Keeley Seafoam** — analog-digital chorus/vibrato. Controls: Rate, Depth, Tone, Blend. Blend dials the relationship between vibrato (fully counter-clockwise) and pure chorus (clockwise); Tone shifts the voicing from mellow/warm to bright shimmer; opto-coupler-controlled LFO gives smooth, deep modulation at slow rates. True bypass.
- **Keeley Hydra** — stereo reverb & tremolo. Reverb side: Dwell (pre-delay/early reflections), Mix (wet/dry), Color (tone-shaping, doubles as secondary-function access when held), 3-way toggle for Spring/Plate/Room. Tremolo side: Rate, Depth, 3-way toggle for Sine/Harmonic/Vibrato waveforms, tap tempo. Independent footswitches for each effect, effect order is switchable (trem→verb or verb→trem), 3 onboard presets, true or buffered trails bypass.
- **Funny Little Boxes 1991 (Gold Sparkle)** — op-amp overdrive, voiced after Pearl Jam's debut-era tone (mid-forward, sparkly top, easy on bass). Controls: 2 Gain knobs, 3-band EQ, Volume.
- **Boss DD-7** — digital delay. Controls: E.Level (effect level), F.Back (feedback/repeats), D.Time (delay time within the selected range), Mode knob (4 delay-time ranges — 50/200/800/3200ms — plus Hold/looper up to 40s, Modulate/chorus-like delay, Analog mode modeled on the BOSS DM-2, and Reverse). Stereo I/O, tap tempo via external footswitch, expression pedal input for real-time control of Time/Feedback/Level.
- **Way Huge Swollen Pickle MK3** — Muff-style fuzz. Controls: Loudness, Filter (tone), Sustain (fuzz/gain) as the three main knobs; Scoop and Crunch as toggle switches on the MK3 (Scoop carves out mids for a huge/flat power-chord voicing; Crunch adjusts compression intensity of the fuzz — both switches down gives the classic 3-knob Swollen Pickle voicing).
- **Mozztronics GD-1 Bonebreaker** — germanium-voiced overdrive, five modes. Best placed early in a chain (sensitive to input impedance, gets fizzy/undefined when stacked after other drives).
- **JHS Cheese Ball** — multi-mode fuzz, Lovetone Big Cheese recreation with four voicings.
- **MXR Super Badass Distortion** — Controls: Output, Distortion (gain), Bass, Mid, Treble — a full 3-band active EQ rather than a single Tone knob, so it can range from low-gain '70s-style overdrive through to scooped modern metal distortion. True bypass.
- **MXR FOD Drive** — dual-channel overdrive recreating two blended amp stacks. Controls: independent Gain/Volume for High Gain and Crunch Gain channels, Blend (mix ratio between channels), Tone, and a Scoop switch (flat/scooped/boosted midrange).
- **Keeley Compressor Mini** — Controls: Level (output volume) and Comp (compression threshold) only — attack, release, and ratio are fixed internally. Uses parallel/auto-blend compression (dry signal passes through and sums with the compressed signal) plus an automatic tone-recovery circuit to retain high end at heavy compression settings. Unity gain typically falls around 30-40% of the Level knob's range.
- **Fender Santa Ana Overdrive** — Controls: Drive, Bass, Middle, Treble, Presence, Output Level, Voice toggle (A/B — A is tighter/natural overdrive, B is smoother/thicker for leads), plus a Boost footswitch (mode set internally to boost either Drive or Level).
- **Warm Audio Warm Bender** — Tone Bender MkII-style fuzz, three NOS germanium/silicon modes (new, in transit)

# Amps

### Frost Amplification Clapp 15
Hand-built, Hobart/Margate, Tasmania (Tim Krushka). 1x12" Class A push-pull combo with a genuine Constant Current Source (CCS) keeping the output stage true Class A — most "Class A" push-pull amps are actually A/B without a CCS.

**Controls:**
- **Class A / Class A-B switch** — Class A limits output to 9W (more compression/sag, breaks up earlier); Class A/B unlocks the full 15W.
- **Bass, Mid, Treble** — standard 3-band EQ.
- **Bass defeat switch** — bypasses the 3-band EQ for a hairier, louder, more open voicing with sparklier highs and more punch.
- **Bright switch toggle** — boosts treble at lower volumes.
- **Pre-Amp (Gain) and Master Volume** — independently controllable, so the clean-to-overdrive transition can be dialed in at any loudness level.
- **Tremolo** — switchable on the front panel or via footswitch (tube-driven).
- **Effects Loop** — for loop-based pedals (EB-10, Brigade, Starlight, Golden Reverberator, RC-5 in the current setup).

**Circuit notes:** Cascode preamp input stage (cascaded triode) — gives Vox/pentode-style dynamics and vibrancy without the feedback/microphony issues real pentode preamps suffer. 12AX7 and 12AU7 preamp valves, EL84 (or Russian 6P14P) output valves. Single channel, no built-in reverb. 12" lightweight neodymium speaker on-board.

### Quilter Aviator Cub
Solid-state (Quilter's amp-modeling power/preamp technology), 1x12" combo, 50W into a custom Quilter/Eminence-designed 12" speaker. Single-channel architecture, but with **three separate input jacks**, each voiced after a different vintage Fender combo — you plug into the input matching the voice you want rather than switching a toggle.

**Voices (selected by input jack):**
- **Tweed** — based on a late-1950s tweed combo. Fat, honest, pre-tone-stack character with plenty of gain on tap for rich overdrive.
- **Blonde** — based on an early-1960s blonde/brown combo. Wider treble boost, clear bell-like tone, more headroom/cleaner than Tweed.
- **Blackface** — based on a 1965 black-panel combo. Scooped mids, sweet silky highs, full bass — often considered the benchmark clean American tone, holds up well pushed into overdrive too.

**Controls:**
- **Gain** — preamp drive level.
- **Bass, Middle, Treble** — active 3-band EQ. Notably strong action: Bass control affects everything below ~500Hz, with the most aggressive effect below 200Hz.
- **Limiter (threshold)** — a "soft ceiling" that caps overdrive tones without killing sustain; unusual on a combo, effectively built-in dynamic control.
- **Reverb** — single-knob level control (onboard digital reverb).
- **Master/Speaker Volume (SPKR VOL)** — power-amp stage output level, independent of preamp gain.
- **SIG OUT** — controls headphone and balanced Line Out volume separately from the speaker.
- **Effects Loop** — send/return, mono.

**Notes:** Lightweight (21.5 lbs) and full-range/flat power section — the "amp" character comes entirely from the three voiced preamp inputs, not from tube sag or output-stage coloration, so it behaves more predictably at any volume than the Clapp 15. Good clean/low-gain pedal platform, especially via the Blackface input for headroom or Tweed input for pre-broken-up grit.

### Kemper Player (Level 3)
Digital amp/effects profiler in a compact pedalboard-style unit (not a physical tube/solid-state amp — outputs into an FRFR speaker, powered cab, PA, or headphones/interface). Uses Kemper's profiling technology to reproduce captured "fingerprints" of real amps rather than modeled approximations.

**Level 3 upgrade specifically adds:** the full signal chain used on the larger Kemper Profiler models — doubles the effects modules from 4 to 8 and adds a Parallel Path (splittable/dual signal routing), plus access to Morphing, more Performance slots, and expanded preset/rig management. The GAIN knob's LED collar glows blue at Level 3 (green = Level I, red = Level II).

**Controls:**
- Two assignable footswitch-adjacent knobs (top left) for quick effect parameter access (e.g., gain/volume of an overdrive), each doubling as a push-button to toggle between two assigned parameters.
- Bluetooth and Wi-Fi activation button (both can run simultaneously — Bluetooth used for play-along audio).
- Row of 6 buttons for Bank/Rig selection, topped with multi-colour indicator LEDs.
- Tap tempo / tuner button.
- Multi-colour input-level LED.
- 3 dedicated tuner LEDs.
- 3 footswitches: left/right navigate rigs (previous/next), centre is assignable to a specific function (e.g. effect on/off).
- Built-in tuner, transpose/pitch feature, USB audio for direct recording, MIDI support, expression pedal input.
- Deep editing and rig organisation via Rig Manager software (Windows/macOS) — the Player's onboard interface is deliberately minimal, so Rig Manager is where most sound design happens.

**Notes:** Profiles (not simulations) of real amps, cabs, and mics — tone accuracy is generally rated very high, with the caveat that the profiled EQ behaviour won't always respond like the real amp's controls would if you push settings far from where it was profiled. Best paired with a genuine FRFR speaker (like the Fender Tone Master FR-10 below) for accurate reproduction.

### Fender Tone Master FR-10 (FRFR monitor)
Powered full-range, flat-response speaker — designed to reproduce whatever the Kemper Player sends it without adding its own amp/cab colouration, unlike a guitar cab or combo. This is the playback partner for the Kemper Player rig, not a tone source in its own right.

**Specs:**
- 1,000W Class-D power amp
- 10" special-design low-frequency driver + 1" wide-dispersion high-frequency compression driver
- Frequency response: 75Hz–20kHz (-3dB); frequency range 58Hz–20kHz (-10dB)
- Max output level: 130dB
- Weight: ~25.6 lbs (11.6kg)

**Controls:** Volume, Treble, Middle, Bass, and a High-Frequency Cut control (3-band active EQ + HF cut) — used to fine-tune the room/stage sound without touching the Kemper's line/PA output. One combo input (balanced 1/4"/XLR), one XLR line out with ground lift, integrated tilt-back legs.

**Notes:** Because it's genuinely flat/full-range, tone shaping should happen on the Kemper (profile selection, onboard EQ, effects) rather than on the FR-10 — the FR-10's own EQ is there for room correction/taste, not for building the tone itself.

# File Organization

*Convention for saving song tone guides to Google Drive (Tone Guides folder).*

**Folder structure:** `Tone Guides/[Artist]/`

**File naming, per song:**
- `[Song] — Tone Guide.html`
- `[Song] — Rhythm Tab.pdf`
- `[Song] — Lead Tab.pdf`

Tabs are saved manually from Ultimate Guitar using the filename above (Claude can't reach into your account or rename files after the fact — it just tells you what to name it when you save).

**Workflow, every time a tone guide is built:**
1. Before building, ask which rig to build for: **Digital** (Kemper Player + Fender Tone Master FR-10), **Clapp 15** (tube, pedals into the amp), or **Quilter Aviator Cub** (solid-state, pedals into the amp). Wait for confirmation, then proceed — don't assume.
2. Once a tone guide is finished, save it to Drive by default — no need to ask each time. Say so if a particular guide shouldn't be saved.
3. Before saving, ask whether YouTube links should be added for that song — tutorial video, live performance — and get the actual link(s) or search and confirm candidates before including. Don't guess.
4. YouTube links (if added) go in the tone guide HTML itself, not as separate files.
5. For any song that already has a saved guide, always ask whether this is a new build or an edit/addition (e.g. adding YouTube links later, updating settings after further testing) before creating a new file or overwriting — never overwrite a dialed-in guide without confirming.
