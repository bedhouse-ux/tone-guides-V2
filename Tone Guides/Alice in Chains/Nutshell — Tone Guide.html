Tone Guide

Nutshell

Alice in Chains — Jar of Flies (1994)
Key: E minor (tuned down ½ step, Eb standard) Rig: Digital — Kemper Player + FR-10 Feel: Dark, spacious, mostly clean grunge ballad

The Reference Point

Jerry Cantrell's part here is subdued and atmospheric — mostly clean with a heavy, dark quality rather than any real gain, plenty of low-end weight, and a lot of room around the guitar. Dynamics come almost entirely from playing and arrangement, not from pedals or amp breakup. The goal is a warm, dark-leaning clean tone with just enough drive on tap for the louder passages, sitting in a spacious, ambient wash rather than up front and aggressive.

The Seymour Duncan '59 neck humbucker with tone rolled off gives exactly the thick, dark humbucking warmth the rig reference describes — a close cousin to the McCarty but slightly darker and thicker, which suits this song's heavy, subdued character better than a brighter single-coil voice. Tone at 6 rather than fully open keeps it warm without going muddy under the Kemper's own EQ shaping.

Profile choice: reach for a dark, warm American or British breakup-leaning profile with real low-end heft — this isn't a scooped, glassy clean like the If You Love Her or Maybe Tomorrow guides, it wants weight and darkness. I don't have visibility into your saved rig library, so pick the darkest/warmest clean-to-edge profile you've got and use these as starting values.

Gain kept low but not fully clean — just enough that the heavier chorus hits round off with a bit of grit rather than staying pristine throughout. Bass pushed up for weight, Middle pulled back for that dark, slightly scooped character, Treble and Presence kept moderate rather than bright.

A tape or analog-style delay algorithm, low feedback, gives the part some space and depth without turning into an obvious rhythmic effect — this is about filling the room, not adding audible repeats.

A longer, darker hall decay than the jangle/pop-rock guides — this is where most of the song's spaciousness comes from. Mix pushed higher than usual so the ambience is genuinely part of the tone, not just a finishing touch.
