Fake Plastic Trees

Radiohead — The Bends (1995)
Key: A (capo 2) Rig: Digital — Kemper Player + FR-10 Feel: Quiet fingerpicked build into a loud strummed peak

The Reference Point

This song's whole arc is dynamic, not textural — quiet, fingerpicked verses that gradually build into a loud, strummed peak near the end before dropping back down. Rather than switching pedals or channels for that shift, the idea here is to set up an amp that reacts to how hard you play: clean and delicate under a light fingerpicked touch, pushing into a bit of natural breakup when you dig in and strum. The Kemper's expressive amp modeling handles that dynamic range more gracefully than an analog pedal-based rig would, which is why this is a Digital rig build.

Tone

Neck pickup for warmth and roundness under the fingerpicked verses — the 58/15 LT pickups give a lively, open low-gain character that stays clear and defined even at a light touch, which matters when you're picking individual notes rather than strumming full chords. Volume wide open so your pick attack is doing all the dynamic work, not the guitar's own volume knob. Tone at 6.5 keeps things warm without getting dull, so the tone still has some life once you dig in for the loud peak.

As with the other Digital-rig guides, the loop-based pedals (Brigade, Starlight, Golden Reverberator, EB-10) live on the Clapp 15's loop, not here. Chorus and reverb come from the Kemper's own onboard effects slots instead. This build leans on the amp block's touch-sensitivity rather than any pedal to get the quiet-to-loud dynamic — no footswitching required mid-song.

Presence

Profile choice: a warm, slightly compressed clean-to-breakup profile — something in the same family as a pushed Vox or a mildly overdriven Fender rather than a scooped ultra-clean or a high-gain amp. As with the other Digital-rig guides, pick your best warm/breakup-leaning profile and use these as starting values rather than an exact match.

Gain set higher than the other clean Digital builds in this library (4 vs. the low-3s used on If You Love Her and Maybe Tomorrow) — this song needs more headroom to react when you dig in for the loud strummed section, rather than staying glassy-clean no matter how hard you play. Fingerpicked verses should stay clear and mostly clean at a light touch; strumming hard for the peak should push it into real, audible breakup.
