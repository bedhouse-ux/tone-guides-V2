Tone Guide

Creep

Radiohead — Pablo Honey (1993)
Key: G Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Quiet, clean verse with sudden distorted chord stabs

The Reference Point

Jonny Greenwood famously said he hated the song enough during rehearsal that he tried to sabotage it, slamming two loud, distorted power-chord stabs into the quiet, clean verse — and the band kept it. That contrast is the entire tone story here: a hushed, fairly clean rhythm sound for the verse chord progression, interrupted by two sudden, harsh, distorted chord hits before the line "I'm a creep" each time, then the whole thing opens up into a fuller distorted chorus. The tone needs to jump, not ease, between those two states.

Tone

Seymour Duncan '59 humbuckers give the H150 real snarl on tap when pushed, which is exactly what those distorted stabs and the chorus need, while still cleaning up reasonably well for the verse with the amp backed off. Bridge pickup throughout — simpler than switching mid-song, and the tonal jump between verse and stabs is handled by pedal engagement, not pickup choice. Tone rolled back slightly from full (6) to keep the clean verse warm rather than glassy; the humbucker's natural thickness carries the distorted sections just fine at this same setting.

The 385 stays bypassed for the entire verse and gets stomped on right before each "I'm a creep" line for the two loud chord hits, then again for the chorus. This footswitch timing is the whole trick of the song — practice the stomp along with the chord change so it lands exactly on the hit, not slightly after.

Bypassed for the verse, stomped on for the two loud chord stabs before each chorus and for the chorus itself. 385+ voicing for the extra gain stage — this needs to feel genuinely harsh and aggressive on the stabs, closer to Greenwood's reported "trying to ruin the take" intent than a polite crunch. Gain and Volume both set high so the jump from clean verse to distorted hit is dramatic and sudden, not a gentle lean-in. Treble and Bass left roughly centered so the pedal doesn't need re-tweaking between the short stabs and the fuller chorus.

Class A/B for a genuinely clean, low-compression base — this needs real headroom so the verse stays hushed and controlled rather than lightly breaking up on its own, which would blunt the contrast when the 385 kicks in. Pre-Amp Gain kept low for the same reason: the amp itself should stay out of the way and let the pedal do 100% of the work of the distorted sections. EQ left fairly neutral so the tonal jump reads as a gain-and-character change, not an EQ change.
