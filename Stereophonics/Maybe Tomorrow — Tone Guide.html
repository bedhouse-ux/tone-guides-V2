Tone Guide

Maybe Tomorrow

Stereophonics — You Gotta Go There to Come Back (2003)
Key: G minor Rig: Digital — Kemper Player + FR-10 Feel: Warm, soulful, wah-coloured build

The Reference Point

Kelly Jones's tone here is warm and vocal rather than aggressive — a soulful alt-rock ballad that builds from an intimate, slightly wah-coloured arpeggiated intro into a fuller, gently driven chorus with horns. This is the guide where the Kemper's expression pedal earns its keep: rather than approximating a wah with a static EQ trick like on Immigrant Song, this rig actually has a real, sweepable wah on tap.

58/15 LT pickups — warm, open, focused midrange rather than scooped or glassy, which suits this song's soulful character better than a brighter single-coil set. Neck pickup for the intro/verse arpeggios, where you want roundness and warmth under the wah coloring. Switch to bridge for the chorus, where the song opens up and needs more push and definition against the fuller arrangement.

Kemper Player — Wah (Expression Pedal)

The Star of This Build

Wah Module

Type: Vox-style sweep
Control: Expression pedal (real-time)
Full sweep
Range

Assign the Wah module to your expression pedal input in Rig Manager so it tracks pedal position in real time, the same way a physical wah would. For the intro and verses, don't treat it like a classic funk wah-wah — rock it gently, holding it partway through its sweep rather than pumping it, to get that vocal, slightly nasal coloring on the arpeggiated riff. Ease off toward the toe-up (or a neutral mid-position) for the chorus so the fuller sound isn't fighting a wah sweep on top of everything else.

This is a genuine advantage of the Digital rig for this particular song — the EB-10 substitute used for Immigrant Song approximates a fixed wah position with a static EQ boost, but here the Kemper's expression pedal gives you the real, continuously variable sweep Kelly Jones would have had.

Presence

Profile choice: something in the warm British breakup family — an edge-of-breakup Vox or early Marshall-style profile fits this song's soulful, slightly gritty-but-clean character better than a scooped American clean or a high-gain profile. As with the Tokyo Tea Room guide, I don't have visibility into your saved rig library, so pick your best warm/breakup-leaning profile and use these as starting values rather than an exact match.

Gain set for a genuine edge-of-breakup character — clean on light touch, breaking up warmly when you dig in, which suits the song's dynamic build from quiet verses to a fuller chorus.

A light overdrive/boost-style stompbox module, engaged for the chorus only, pushes the amp block a bit harder and adds some level for the bigger sections without changing the fundamental character. Kept low-gain — this is a push, not a distinct drive tone.
