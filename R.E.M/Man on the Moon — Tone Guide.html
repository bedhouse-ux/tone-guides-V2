Man on the Moon

R.E.M. — Automatic for the People (1992)
Key: G Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Bright, open, jangly clean strum

The Reference Point

Peter Buck's tone here is clean, bright, and open — a straightforward strummed jangle that carries the verse's rolling chord shapes without any grit or push. This isn't a moody or atmospheric clean tone like the dream-pop builds elsewhere in the library; it's a fairly direct, present strum that sits comfortably up front in a fairly sparse arrangement. Nothing fancy in the pedal chain — the tone is really about picking dynamics and getting the chord voicings clean.

Guitar

Yamaha SA2200

Pickup Selector: Middle (both pickups)
Coil-Split: Engaged (both pickups)

Tone

The SA2200's coil-split gives access to a brighter, thinner, more single-coil-like voice than its full humbucker mode — closer to the chiming, slightly glassy character this song's open-chord strum wants than the fuller, darker semi-hollow tone would give. Both pickups selected and both split for maximum brightness and jangle. Volume wide open, Tone at 7 to keep some warmth in reserve so the strum doesn't get thin or brittle.

Class A/B for clean headroom — this needs to stay genuinely clean through the verse strumming, not lightly break up. Bright switch on and Treble pushed up together for the chiming top end that pairs with the split-coil guitar tone; Bass kept moderate so the open chords don't get boomy. Pre-Amp Gain kept low, just enough that hard strums have a touch of natural amp response without any real breakup.
