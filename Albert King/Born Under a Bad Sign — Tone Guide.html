Born Under a Bad Sign

Albert King — Born Under a Bad Sign (1967)
Key: C# minor (12-bar blues) Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Thick, vocal, touch-driven blues lead

The Reference Point

Albert King's tone here is searing but not heavily distorted — the character comes almost entirely from his unorthodox playing (huge bends, played left-handed on a right-handed Flying V) and a warm, thick, vocal-sounding amp rather than a gain-driven pedal chain. The goal is a touch-responsive setup where digging in produces real breakup and easing off cleans it back up — dynamics from the hands and the amp working together, not a fixed distortion level.

The 58/15 LT neck pickup's vintage PAF-style warmth and focused midrange gives the thick, vocal quality this song wants — closer to the sweetness of old single coils crossed with humbucker warmth than a scooped modern voicing, which suits Albert King's singing lead tone well. Tone at 7 keeps it rounded without going dark, so bends still cut through.

The classic Klon-style move: push the front of an already-breaking-up amp rather than supply distortion on its own. Gain at 4 keeps this a transparent boost with a little grit, not a distinct drive tone — the amp's own Class A breakup is doing the real work. Mids nudged up slightly for that vocal, present blues midrange.

Class A mode is the whole point of this build — the earlier breakup and sag mean picking dynamics do real work: dig in for grit, back off for near-clean. Gain set moderately high so the amp is genuinely on the edge of breakup even before the Tumnus pushes it further. Mid pushed up for that thick, vocal blues midrange; Bass Defeat and Bright both off so the amp stays warm and rounded rather than open and sparkly — this tone wants weight, not top-end bite.
