Tone Guide

Here Comes Your Man

Pixies — Doolittle (1989)
Key: D Rig: Clapp 15 Feel: Bright, jangly, headroom-forward pop-rock

The Reference Point

Joey Santiago's part here is almost the opposite of the Pixies' usual squall — clean-to-barely-breaking-up, bright and chiming, closer to a Byrds or Buddy Holly jangle than the band's noisier work. The goal is clarity and sparkle over grit: an amp with real headroom, doing the character work through EQ shaping rather than sag or saturation.

Guitar

Postmodern Telecaster

The middle switch position combines the Nocaster '51 bridge and CuNiFe neck pickups — the classic Tele jangle voicing, rounder and fuller than bridge-only twang but still articulate. Volume wide open. Tone at 7 rather than fully open keeps the top end chiming instead of icepick-y, since the amp's Bright switch is already adding sparkle downstream.

Spring-style voicing fits the bright, surf-adjacent character of this part. Kept short — a hint of size and shimmer, not an obvious effect. Keep the Brigade and Starlight off; this part doesn't want modulation or delay stacked on top.

Class A/B for full headroom — this song wants clean-to-just-breaking-up clarity, not the earlier sag Class A gives you on the fuzz/blues guides. Gain kept moderate-low so the amp stays clean on light touch with just a little push on hard strums. Bass Defeat stays off since the 3-band EQ is doing real shaping work here — Mid and Treble both pushed up for chime and presence, not a scooped voicing. Bright switch on for extra sparkle at lower stage volumes, reinforcing the jangle character from the guitar and amp EQ together.
