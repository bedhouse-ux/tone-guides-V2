Are You Gonna Go My Way

Lenny Kravitz — Are You Gonna Go My Way (1993)
Key: E minor pentatonic Rig: Digital — Kemper Player + FR-10 Feel: Hendrix-indebted fuzz-and-wah rock

The Reference Point

Kravitz cut this through a fuzz pedal into a cranked Marshall-plexi-style amp, with wah reserved for the lead lines rather than run constantly — critics at the time singled out the intro's "blast of phase-shifter guitar," directly comparing it to Hendrix and the Isley Brothers. That phase-shifter detail matters: there's no phaser pedal anywhere in the current collection, board or shelf, so this build gets the fuzz-and-wah backbone right but can't fully reproduce that specific intro texture. Noted here rather than papered over.

58/15 LT humbuckers give a warm, open, vintage-leaning voice with plenty of output to slam into the Warm Bender — closer to the thick Les Paul/Flying V-family tones Kravitz favored than anything single-coil in the collection. Volume wide open for a full, consistent signal into the fuzz. Tone just barely rolled back from full to soften the top end slightly before the fuzz gets hold of it.

Signal Chain

Fender Tone Master FR-10
The Warm Bender comes off the shelf and sits in front of the Kemper's input — a physical fuzz pedal feeding a digital amp, same logic as a stompbox into any amp. Fuzz goes first: putting anything ahead of a germanium fuzz's input (a wah included) can load down its input impedance and thin out the tone, so the Kemper's own wah — placed after the fuzz in the digital signal path — avoids that problem entirely while still giving you a real, continuously variable expression-pedal sweep rather than a static approximation.

The Version rotary's Silicon mode is the call here rather than either NOS germanium mode — silicon fuzz circuits generally read as more aggressive and modern, with less of the sag and "dying battery" compression that the NOS 75 brought to the Zeppelin-era Whole Lotta Love build. That harder, more direct character suits a 1993 rock record better than a vintage Beck/Ronson-style voicing. Attack set high for a full wall-of-fuzz saturation on the riff. Level around unity so the fuzz doesn't jump ahead of the amp block's own gain staging.

Assigned to the expression pedal in Rig Manager, same setup as the Maybe Tomorrow build. Keep it out of the main riff — the reference for this song uses wah specifically on solos and lead phrasing, not as a constant rhythm-guitar texture. Halo-style voicing for the vintage, vocal-forward sweep that fits a Hendrix-adjacent song like this one; the brighter Red Fasel character would push this in a more modern direction than the era calls for.

Gain kept moderate rather than maxed — the Warm Bender is doing the heavy saturation work, so the amp block just needs to add width and push rather than a second full gain stage on top of the fuzz.

Kemper Player — Effects

Approximation — Flagged Gap

Phaser (onboard effects slot)

The documented intro tone is described specifically as phase-shifter guitar. If your Kemper's stock effects include a baseline Phaser module (typically included separately from the Level 3 upgrade's added effects), engage it lightly on the intro only — slow rate, moderate depth, modest mix. I can't confirm from here whether that module is present on this specific unit; worth a quick check in Rig Manager before the session. If it's not available, this is an honest gap in the build rather than something to fake with the Brigade's vibrato, which is a different kind of modulation and won't read the same way.
